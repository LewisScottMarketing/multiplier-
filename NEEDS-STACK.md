# Needs Stack — eduleaders compliance tool

Whose stack this is: **Group 1** — a school or Multi-Academy Trust (MAT) in England that today runs staff vetting from spreadsheets. The person in the chair is the one who runs the Single Central Record (SCR) day to day: usually the school business manager or school HR, with the head teacher accountable above them. Schools switching from an existing SCR system are not on this stack; they are N1 occupants to win from. Product: a KCSiE safer-recruitment tool that handles job application submissions and keeps the SCR up to date for DBS and other checks.

Unvetted anchor: no ideal-customer definition (CAROL.md), interview findings, or keystones chart exist yet for this product. This stack is built on a customer sketch plus desk research. Levels are numbered in settle order; position in the list below carries the stack order, top ends before bottom means.

## The stack

**N5.** ★ Do the job I went into education for, knowing I did everything I needed to keep children safe, so I can switch off at the end of the day without dreading an inspection or an incident. — *purpose level: no product satisfies this directly; material for higher-purpose stories, never for promises.*

**N4.** → Run a school where no child is harmed by an adult we hired, and parents know it.
    Occupants: safeguarding training providers; the school's designated safeguarding lead; concern-recording systems such as CPOMS. (Unverified.)
    Role: aspiration — show the part you play; never promise it.
    Why your level still wins for your ideal customer: those cover what happens once staff are in the building; the tool covers who gets in and whether the record of it is right.

**N3.** → Meet our KCSiE safer-recruitment duties on every hire, so an inspector finds nothing wrong when they check.
    Occupants: local authority safeguarding audit and support teams; external safeguarding auditors and consultants; a trust's own central compliance team.
    Role: **the promise** — the outcome claimed as a consequence of N1. KCSiE is statutory guidance schools must follow, so this is a must-have, not an aspiration.
    Why your level still wins for your ideal customer: audits and consultants check at a point in time and report gaps; the tool keeps the records right every day, so there is little for an audit to find. (Draft.)

**N1.** → Know that every member of staff has all their required checks done and in date, and be able to show the record on demand.
    Occupants: you; CPOMS (StaffSafe module); School SCR / MAT SCR; Online SCR; The Access Group (HR suite with SCR module).
    Role: **what you do** — features live here; meet searchers where they are.  ← YOU ARE HERE

**N2.** → Collect application forms and references by email or paper, track each hire's vetting checks in a spreadsheet, and chase whatever is missing by hand.
    Occupants: Excel and Google Sheets; emailed or paper application forms; email and phone for references; shared drives (e.g. SharePoint); the DBS Update Service portal; job boards and recruitment platforms (e.g. TES — unverified).
    Role: advancement — steps you make obsolete; brag about them.

## Uses beyond positioning

Measure success one level up from what you deliver: not "records kept," but whether schools reach inspection with nothing found (N3). The purpose levels (N4, N5) are where the company's higher purpose lives: stories of a business manager who stopped dreading the inspector's call are worth telling. Moving the product up a level, for example into wider KCSiE compliance, is a different business; partial climbs (training content, audit-readiness checklists) are the realistic versions.
